# Content Evaluator

## What This Does
Runs content through four evaluation lenses: a marketing strategist
debate, a busy subscriber test, a substance scanner, and a skimmer's
path analysis.

## When to Use This
- Before publishing blog posts or newsletter editions
- When content feels "done" but you're not sure it lands
- To pressure-test hooks and structure

## What to Expect
- Identification of where readers might disengage
- Specific quotes with "deletion moments"
- Marketing strategist perspective on positioning
- Busy subscriber verdict: would they finish reading?
- Flagged sections that fail the "so what?" test with recommended actions
- Skimmer's path analysis: whether headers alone carry the argument

## Boundaries
Does NOT provide grammar or spelling checks
Does NOT rewrite your content
Does NOT validate factual accuracy
Does NOT evaluate SEO

## How to Invoke
Provide the full content and target audience.
Specify if you want line-by-line or big-picture feedback.

## Evaluation Framework

### Lens 1: Marketing Strategist Debate
Two strategists argue over the content and are judged by a third observer:
- **Advocate**: Defends the piece — what works, what resonates
- **Critic**: Challenges assumptions — what's missing, what falls flat
- **Verdict**: Tells who won the debate and how to improve it

### Lens 2: Busy Subscriber Test
Simulates a reader who:
- Has 47 unread emails
- Gives you 8 seconds to earn their attention
- Will close the tab the moment interest drops

The test identifies:
- **Hook strength**: Would they keep reading past the first paragraph?
- **Deletion moments**: Exact points where attention would drop
- **Finish probability**: Honest odds they'd read to the end

### Lens 3: Substance Scanner
Reviews content for substance by asking after each major section: "Why should a consultant, coach, manager, or business owner care about this specific point?"

If the answer can't be stated in one sentence, the section is flagged.

The test identifies:
- **Section relevance**: Can the value be stated in one sentence?
- **Flagged sections**: Sections that fail the "so what?" test
- **Recommended action**: Cut, connect more clearly to reader pain, or reposition

### Lens 4: Skimmer's Path
Reads only headers, subheaders, and first sentences of each paragraph — the skeleton a skimmer would see.

The test evaluates:
- **Argument clarity**: Does the skeleton alone make the argument clear?
- **Forward momentum**: Do headers create a pull to keep reading?
- **Skimmer value**: Does a skimmer walk away with something useful?
- **Header failures**: Which specific headers fail and why, with restructuring suggestions
